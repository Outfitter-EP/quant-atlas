How an inflation zero curve is bootstrapped

From swap quotes to a CPI curve. Every chart below reacts to the sliders. All numbers are illustrative, not live market data.

The idea

Traders quote zero-coupon inflation swaps (ZCIS) for 1, 2, 3 ... 30 years. Each quote bT is a promise: CPI will grow by bT per year, compounded, over T years. Turn each quote into a CPI level, put the levels in a row, and you have the inflation curve. The zero rates, forwards and monthly values are all read off that row.

I(T) = I0 × (1 + bT)T  CPI level at T
z(T) = ( I(T) / I0 )1/T − 1  = bT  zero inflation rate
f(T1,T2) = ( I(T2) / I(T1) )1/(T2−T1) − 1  forward inflation

It is called bootstrapping because you build outward from the short end and each new point uses only points you already have. With ZCIS each step is a one-line formula. With year-on-year swaps each step needs a solve, shown at the end.

Terms in one breath

CPI
The consumer price index. One number that tracks the price level, published monthly.
Base CPI, I0
The CPI level the swaps start from. It is the latest known fixing, shifted by the lag.
Lag
Swaps pay on CPI from a few months earlier, typically 3 (US, EUR) or 2 (UK RPI). So the curve sits on a "reference CPI" timeline, not on payment dates.
ZCIS
A swap with one payment at maturity. One side pays total CPI growth, the other pays (1+b)T−1.
Breakeven bT
The fixed rate that makes the ZCIS worth zero today. It is the market's average yearly inflation for T years.
Zero inflation rate
Average yearly inflation from today to T. For ZCIS it equals the quote.
Forward inflation
Inflation expected between two future dates, such as year 5 to year 10 (the "5y5y").
Seasonality
Months that are reliably high or low, like energy in winter. It averages to zero over a year.
Nominal rate
The ordinary interest rate used to discount money.
Real rate
Nominal rate minus inflation: (1+n)/(1+b)−1. What you earn after prices rise.

The moves

  1. Fix the base.

    Take the latest CPI the swaps reference, after the lag. Call it I0.

  2. Read the quotes.

    One breakeven per maturity. Gaps between quoted years are filled by interpolating the rates.

  3. Turn each quote into a CPI level.

    I(T) = I0(1+bT)T. Both legs of a ZCIS pay on the same day, so discounting cancels and the nominal curve is not needed here.

  4. Fill the gaps.

    Between two yearly points, grow CPI at a constant rate (geometric interpolation) to get monthly values.

  5. Add seasonality.

    Lay a 12-month pattern on top. It sums to zero, so the yearly points do not move.

Discounting enters afterwards, when you value cash flows or compare with real rates. That is the last chart of the lab.

Bootstrap it year by year

Each new point needs only its own quote and the base. The forward for that year is the new CPI divided by the previous CPI.

Seasonality

Month-on-month CPI change for the 36 months after the July 2026 base. The smooth steps are the trend from the annual points. The wiggle adds the seasonal pattern. Over any 12 months the extra pieces cancel, so each yearly CPI node stays where the quotes put it.

Change the interest rate

The CPI curve and the breakeven line do not move. Real rates move one for one with nominal rates. The dashed year-on-year swap rate moves slightly because discounting changes the weights of the yearly forwards.

Trade inspector

Notional 10,000,000. At inception the two legs have equal expected size, so the swap is worth zero. If inflation averages differently from the quote, the receiver of inflation gains or loses the difference at maturity.

Where bootstrapping becomes a real solve

A year-on-year (YoY) swap pays each year's CPI growth against a fixed rate y. Its value depends on discount factors DFi, so each new maturity solves for one new forward using the ones already found:

Fn = ( yn Σi≤n DFi − Σi<n DFiFi ) / DFn  Fi = I(i)/I(i−1) − 1

This sketch ignores the convexity adjustment that real YoY pricing adds. In practice desks fit one curve to ZCIS, YoY swaps and inflation-linked bonds together, with seasonality and the published CPI fixings at the front.
