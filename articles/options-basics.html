Options from scratch: calls, puts and payoffs

What you buy, what you can lose, and how to read a payoff picture. Pricing starts in step 2.

The idea

An option is a contract that gives its holder a right and no duty. A call is the right to buy an asset at a fixed price. A put is the right to sell it at a fixed price. The fixed price is the strike. The right ends on the expiry date. You pay the seller a premium today for it.

Because the buyer can walk away, the most a buyer can lose is the premium. The seller has the opposite deal: keep the premium, carry the obligation.

Terms in one breath

Underlying
The asset the option is about: a share, an index, a currency, a commodity.
Spot, S
The price of the underlying today. ST is its price at expiry.
Strike, K
The agreed price at which you may buy (call) or sell (put).
Expiry, T
When the right ends. Time left is measured in years.
Premium
The price of the option itself, paid by the buyer.
Long / short
Long means you bought the option. Short means you sold (wrote) it.
European / American
European options can only be used at expiry. American ones any time before.
Payoff
What the option is worth at expiry, before counting the premium.
Moneyness
In the money: exercising now would pay. At the money: S ≈ K. Out of the money: it would not.
Intrinsic value
What exercising right now would pay. Never negative.
Time value
Premium minus intrinsic value. You pay for the chance that things move your way before expiry.

The formulas

Call payoff = max(ST − K, 0)
Put payoff = max(K − ST, 0)
Buyer's profit = payoff − premium
Premium = intrinsic value + time value

Lab 2: combine options

Two or more options on the same asset give payoffs a single option cannot. Each strategy answers a question about what you expect.

Straddle
Call and put at one strike. Profits from a big move either way. Loses if the price stays put.
Strangle
Same idea with strikes apart. Cheaper, but the move must be bigger.
Bull call spread
Buy a call, sell a higher call. Cheaper than the call alone, with profit capped.
Bear put spread
The mirror image for a falling market.
Butterfly
A bet that the price ends near the middle strike. Small cost, small loss, a narrow peak of profit.
Covered call
Own the stock and sell a call. You earn the premium and give up the big upside.
Protective put
Own the stock and buy a put. Insurance: a floor under your loss.
Risk reversal
Sell a put to pay for a call. Upside exposure at low cost, with a downside obligation.
Go deeper: an option is a bet on movement

The payoff of a call has a kink at the strike. Below it the buyer loses a fixed amount. Above it the gain keeps growing. Losses are capped, gains are not, so an option always gains from extra movement, whichever way the price moves. This is why the price of an option depends on how much the asset moves (its volatility) and not on which way you think it will go.

It is also why the premium is not the average payoff. You cannot simply ask for the expected payoff under your own beliefs, because a trader can copy the option with the stock and cash. The price is the cost of that copy. Step 2 builds it.
